## 1. Responsible parties and project — MiCA Annex I
HGW is a proposed network for commerce, cooperative digital services, and planned energy, manufacturing, education and health services. The existing Eco-smart-mall codebase is a **demonstrator**. No operational capacity or service availability beyond validated deployed functionality is represented by this draft.

**Commercial operations:** The user supplied a Swedish Tax Agency extract dated 2026-10-07 in the name Elrefaey Same showing Swedish VAT registration, SNI 47.123 for online/mail-order retail, A-skatt and employer status. This extract does not itself establish an incorporated legal-person issuer, F-skatt authorisation, or a MiCA financial-services permission. Sensitive personal-registration and street-address data have deliberately not been copied into this public repository.

**Future group:** HGW Global Holding AB and a nonprofit foundation with an intended 75% shareholding are **future plans, not incorporated and evidenced issuers**. A separate political party is intended to be legally and financially independent.

**CRITICAL LEGAL GATE:** MiCA Article 4(1)(a) requires an **offeror that is a legal person** for a non-exempt public offer of other crypto-assets. A Swedish enskild firma / sole proprietorship does not constitute a separate legal person. Therefore the current proprietor is **not presented here as an eligible Title II public-offer legal-person offeror**. An actual Swedish legal-person offeror, or a separately documented lawful exception, must be established and classified first. Do not submit this file naming an unincorporated planned AB as the issuer.

Before filing, identify legal name and registration number, corporate seat, authorisation/signing capacity, management members, ownership/control, audited or appropriate financial information, material contracts, solvency, other activities and possible conflicts.

## 2. Token purpose, rights and limitations
**Working symbol:** HGW-U (not a reserved/issued ticker).  
**Purpose:** Proposed access to specified goods and services **provided by the issuer** under separately enforceable terms. Supply, service coverage, how to redeem credits, consumer purchase price and actual delivery commitments remain **undefined**. Access to third-party merchants' independent goods must be assessed separately: merely allowing purchases from many merchants does **not** establish the MiCA definition of a utility token (access to a good or service supplied by its issuer).

Proposed token **does not** grant equity, shareholder votes, dividend, project profit participation, debt principal or interest, claims on nonprofit assets, preferential shares, green-project returns, collateral, property title, IP ownership, a bank deposit or statutory investor protection. If any term grants those rights, the classification and documentation must change.

**No fixed value:** HVU is a hypothetical internal accounting/reporting comparator only. It is not a fiat reference, pegged token, transferable stablecoin, or commitment to redeem at par. No target exchange rate, guaranteed appreciation, market liquidity or promise of convertibility among shares/sukuk/tokens exists.

**Terms remaining to define:** Service inventory, accepting merchant/issuer, delivery place and timing, expiry if lawful, whether token transferable, eligibility, revocation, fees, refund/reversal/chargeback treatment, failed issuer performance, complaint routes, currency and VAT treatment.

## 4. Underlying technology and cross-chain design
Target networks under evaluation: Arbitrum and Ethereum-compatible networks, Solana, Base, Polygon and BNB Chain. Current repository includes a browser-only simulation with **no deployed audited smart contract, live payment switch, operational bridge or on-chain verified token**.

Desired invariant: every legally and economically outstanding HGW-U unit has one reconciled authoritative supply record across chains; cross-chain transfers never cause double issuance. Potential implementation uses audited burn/mint or escrow/mint controls, chain finality and replay protection, bridge risk limits, role-based access, multisignature administration, emergency pause, independent monitoring and account recovery.

**Before filing:** specify actual chain and consensus mechanisms, smart contract and bridge addresses, source-code audit(s), upgrade controls, vulnerabilities, fees, supported custody, disaster recovery, node dependencies, incidents, technical rights and holder transfer procedures. No bridge technology or third-party provider is guaranteed or endorsed.

## 5. Governance, custody and operational arrangements
The issuer must disclose responsibilities of administrators, voting rights, cap table, related-party service providers, treasury authorisation and who can mint, freeze, upgrade or burn. Project governance votes (if any) are independent of corporate shareholder governance. A person's ownership of tokens may be subject to legal limitations and cannot, by itself, supersede statutory rights.

Identity can rely on appropriate eIDAS-recognised methods, secure customer verification and qualified signatures where needed; no biometrics will be stored on public ledgers. All personal-data processing remains subject to a specific GDPR assessment, lawful basis, retention rules and user rights.

## 6. Principal risks (non-exhaustive)
- **Total loss** of token purchase consideration due to product failure, bankruptcy, adverse law, fraud or lack of demand.
- No guaranteed listing, market, liquidity, transfer, redemption or access to planned services.
- Unknown project capital and budget, undisclosed solvency and execution risks until issuer financials are completed.
- Token contract exploits; insecure upgrades and privileged keys; bridge hacks, chain reorganisations, downtime, consensus attack, validator concentration and censorship.
- Wrong-chain transfer, irrecoverable private-key loss, custody failure, phishing or compromised account.
- Value volatility; price manipulation; conflicts of interest; illicit-market abuse.
- Tax consequences; cross-border and sanctions restrictions; unanticipated obligations.
- Climate/energy impact of consensus mechanisms remains to be quantified; “green” branding is not evidence.
- AML/KYC, data-breach, consumer-rights and payment-provider failure.
